Generated by `node simulations/run.js` on 2026-11-07 · engine v2.3 · 10 CPU workers · 10 min.

**7,200,000 training holes** for the learning agent, then **137,640 simulated 18-hole bot rounds** on one fixed par-70 course.

## Headlines

- **Tier spread:** a full Bronze bag plays **+1.11** per hole against par, a full Diamond bag **-0.47** (learned agent). That's **28.4 strokes per round** between the two.
- **A real game** (start at Bronze, buy upgrades between holes): the evolved shopper averages **74.3** (+4.32 vs par), the game's own CPU shopping **80.4**, the expert planning bots **71.6**.
- **Progression:** holes 1–6 average **+0.91**, holes 13–18 **-0.58** per hole (evolved shopper). By the last hole the bag averages tier **2.23**.
- **Best strategy:** RL evolved shopper (74.3, wins 68.5% of 4-player tables). Worst: Short game (wedges + putter) (98.7).
- **Play time:** about **4.1 swings per player per hole** (2.6 long shots + 1.4 putts). See the play-time section.

## How the simulation works

- **Rules:** the real engine (`prototype/engine.js`): 2d6 distance strip, 2d6 direction fan, tier-scaled distance (75% → 125%), lie drops, ring putting, coin tokens. Economy mirrors `prototype/game.js`: birdie 5 / par 3 / bogey 2 / worse 1 coins, tier costs 1-2-2-3, 6-card shop (4 upgrades + 2 jokers, 40% sale chance, $1 reroll), jokers and tickets.
- **Learning agent (shots + putts):** tabular Q-learning. State = bag tier band, lie, distance to pin (15-yd buckets), where water/OB is (none / left / right / both). Actions = every legal club (and Driver power) × aim (−10°, 0°, +10°); on the green SAFE or RISKY per ring. Reward = −1 per stroke (penalty strokes included). Trained on random holes plus the course, in parallel workers whose tables are merged each round.
- **Learning shopper:** a cross-entropy-method search over shop weights (how much it wants each club group, the putter, each joker, coin reserve, reroll threshold), scored on full 18-hole games with the learned agent swinging.
- **Opponents:** 8 hand-written shop archetypes and the engine's own planning bot ("expert", evaluates every club × aim with exact dice odds).
- **Jokers that need a human choice** (Caddie, Mulligan) use the game's CPU rule: reroll when the shot goes wide or is penalised.

## 7. Balancing notes

- **Tiers matter a lot.** Bronze → Diamond is worth 28.4 strokes per round. That is a big gap: an early lead in upgrades snowballs. Consider making Silver a smaller step or slowing coin income.
- **Starting difficulty.** A full Bronze bag plays +1.11 per hole (20.0 over par for a round). Hard but playable: bogey golf at the start, par golf once upgraded.
- **Upgrades are worth buying.** Never buying anything costs 15.7 strokes against the evolved shopper; the hoarder wins 10.8% of tables.
- **Strategy diversity.** Among strategies that buy, the gap from best to worst is 24.4 strokes per round (top: RL evolved shopper 74.3, Game CPU (random joker + cheapest) 80.4, Putter first 84.0). One build is clearly ahead; see whether its favourite cards are under-priced.
- **Jokers.** Best: steady (+4.84 strokes/18, 1.21 per coin). Weakest: roughrider (-2.16). steady, laser, bighitter, caddie look under-priced (more than 0.6 strokes per coin); mulligan, jail, roughrider barely change a round or hurt (within noise).
- **Direction.** Straight shots go from 16.7% (Bronze) to 83.4% (Diamond), close to the cards' 17% → 83%. Penalty strokes per hole drop from 0.10 to 0.05.
- **Club variety.** The Driver is 29.8% of long shots; i8, i5, i7, i6 are almost never used (<2%). Many near-duplicate clubs: the 12-card bag could shrink without losing decisions.
- **Economy.** A round earns about 57.8 coins (1.1 from tokens and jokers). Maxing all 13 cards costs 104, so players finish around tier 2.23: progression is slow; consider more coins or cheaper tiers.
- **Putting.** The learned agent putts RISKY 80.7% of the time and averages 1.42 putts per hole. Both choices get used, so the SAFE/RISKY decision is live.
- **Rough Rider is a trap card (-2.16 strokes).** The rough "drop" pushes every result one step shorter, so short and on-target both land on the *same* distance. Rough shots become almost fixed-distance (7-iron: 120 yd on 2–11, 130 only on 12), which is *more* predictable than the fairway. Players just club up. Removing the drop brings back the spread, so the joker hurts. Fix idea: make the rough cost direction instead (play one tier worse on the direction strip), or let the drop also add a step of distance spread.
- **Lob Wedge as a chipper.** The Lob Wedge is 33.3% of long shots (mostly 30–50 yd chips onto the green), while the 5–8 irons together are 4.5%. Irons are 10 yd apart and the short/long notch is also 10 yd, so neighbouring irons overlap almost completely. Consider a smaller bag (e.g. 4, 6, 8 irons) or 20-yd gaps.
- **The first hole is brutal (+2.79 vs par).** A Bronze Driver goes 190 yd, so a 348-yd par 4 needs three long shots. Start the course with a short par 3/4, or count par from the Bronze distances for the opening holes.
- **The game's CPU shopping is weak** (6.0 strokes worse than the evolved shopper). It buys a random joker and then the cheapest upgrade. A better CPU: Driver and putter first, then woods; take Steady Hands / Laser Putter when offered; skip wedge upgrades.
- **Late coins are worth little.** Piggy Bank earns +39.17 coins per round but saves only +0.37 strokes, because those coins arrive late, when there are few holes left to use the upgrades. Coin jokers (Piggy, Lucky 7, Magpie) need to pay out early or be cheaper.
